## A Short Introduction to Trello and Its Core Features
![Trello Banner
](https://fluentboards.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/Screenshot-2024-04-15-at-3.38.19 PM-2048x920-1-1024x460.webp)
[Trello](<https://trello.com/>), developed by **Trello Enterprise in 2011** (a subsidiary of Atlassian). It’s like your digital whiteboard! Its simple and user-friendly project management features help you organize your tasks and projects in a fun and visual way.
Here’s what makes it awesome:
  * **Boards:** Trello organizes projects into boards. Each board represents a project or a specific area of work.

  * **Lists:** Within each board, you can create lists to represent different stages or categories of work. For example, you might have lists for “To Do,” “In Progress,” and “Completed.

  * **Cards:** Tasks or items are represented as cards within lists. Cards can contain detailed information such as descriptions, checklists, due dates, attachments, and comments.

  * **Drag-and-Drop Interface:** Trello’s intuitive drag-and-drop interface allows users to easily move cards between lists, reorder tasks, and change priorities.

  * **Labels and Tags:** Trello offers labels and tags to help categorize and prioritize tasks. Users can assign colors and names to labels, making it easy to identify and filter tasks.

  * **Assignees:** You can assign cards to specific team members, making it clear who is responsible for each task. This promotes accountability and collaboration within the team.

  * **Notifications:** Trello sends notifications to users when changes are made to cards they’re involved in, ensuring everyone stays informed about project updates.

  * **Integrations:** Trello integrates with various third-party apps allowing users to connect their Trello boards with other tools they use regularly.

## Why Look for Trello Alternatives?
When it comes to project management, every project presents its unique challenges. And, people turn to different project management tools to address these challenges.
But what if the tool itself becomes a problem?
Unfortunately, that’s what happened when Trello announced “**Free Workspaces in Trello will soon be limited to 10 collaborators’!**
This rule will be applicable starting **April 8, 2024,** and it will affect everyone, whether they are workspace members, guests, or pending invitations.
![Announcement of Trello's member limitation
](https://fluentboards.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/image-1024x244.webp)
Project management tools are meant to help you cut your project costs, right? But with this declaration, **the room for expenses has only increased**. It’s a speed bump on the way to scalability!
So, the quest for a Trello alternative became a necessity for any business that has a team of more than 10 members!
However, it’s just one reason; others might instigate the decision to switch tools. Here are some of them:
  * **Lack of WordPress integration:** Trello doesn’t have any direct integration with WordPress. So, if you are a WordPress user, using Trello can be an extra bit of a hassle for you, as you have to switch platforms to manage your projects.

  * **Security concerns:** For teams dealing with sensitive information or working in regulated industries, Trello’s security features might not be enough. This often leads them to search for alternatives with stronger security measures

  * **Complexity for non-technical users:** Trello comes with a ton of features. But more isn’t always better, and sometimes it works otherwise. Especially for a non-tech guy, some of Trello’s features might seem a bit complex and confusing.

  * **Over-reliance on third-party integrations:** Trello is over-dependent on third-party integrations. While these integrations extend its functionality, they can also be a drawback for some users, especially when you prefer having essential features built-in.

  * **Large gap between free and pro versions:** The gap between the free and the pro version’s features is huge, making it tough for small businesses and limiting their possibilities to scale.
