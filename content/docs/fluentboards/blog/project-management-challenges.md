## Project Management Challenges and Solutions
A [project life cycle](<https://fluentboards.com/blog/phases-of-project-management/>) consists of many crucial stages. Each has its own set of challenges.
Here are some common project management challenges project managers face while they [manage a project team](<https://fluentboards.com/blog/how-to-manage-a-project-team/>) –
### Lack of clear goals & objectives
Having a super clear vision is key to achieving success. If the vision is unclear, it can turn a project into a mess.
_Lack of clear project goals contributes to[37% of project failure](<https://blog.kintone.com/business-with-heart/why-projects-fail-unclear-objectives-and-goals/>)!_
But wait, there’s more to it than just having clear goals. Even if your goals are clear, they need to be realistic too. That’s where project managers need to work smartly by setting goals that are not just clear but also achievable.
**Best practices:**
A quantifying project approach can help you move closer to your project goals. So, 
  * Break your business goal into project milestones and design a detailed [project management workflow](<https://fluentboards.com/blog/project-management-workflow/>)
  * List exactly what needs to be done within the project and create a [project to-do list](<https://fluentboards.com/blog/project-management-to-do-list/>) for individuals and teams
  * Don’t forget to back up your goals with clear objectives for team progress
  * Go with the SMART(specific, measurable, attainable, relevant, and time-bound) and CLEAR (collaborative, limited, emotional, appreciable, and refinable) approach
  * Pick any of these approaches or blend both of them to reach your target

### Scope Creep
Scoop Creep is perhaps the biggest project management challenge for any project manager. It simply means – **simultaneously** **changing the requirements of a project from its originally agreed state**.
This scenario occurs when clients don’t know what they want and have vague goals and objectives risking the overall aspect of project success.
**Best practices:**
To avoid scope creep-like scenarios, project managers can take the following steps:
  * Engaging with clients during the initial project planning phase
  * Put the requirements, goals, and objectives down to contacts
  * Apply agile project management approaches like Scrum to help your team progress
  * During the implementation phase keep track of deadlines, and team progress using a project management tool
  * Make sure to keep an effective change control process in your plan

### Lack of communication
For successful project completion, ensuring effective communication within your [project team](<https://fluentboards.com/blog/what-is-a-project-team/>) is key. Miscommunication affects the overall teamwork.
[_56% of projects fail_](<https://www.pmi.org/learning/library/communication-method-content-in-project-9937>) _because of the need for more communication between team members and the project leader!_
Moreover, “Miscommunication”, and “Misunderstanding” are also hazardous for a project. It can cause team conflicts and contradictions that can potentially delay or lead to a project’s failure.
**Best practices:**
To deal with a lack of communication, project managers can:
  * Incorporate centralized project management tools such as a medium of collaboration
  * Regular short stand-up meetings can improve collaboration

### Team workload mismanagement and weaker collaboration
This project management challenge perfectly aligns with the aforementioned point. Miscommunication leads to mismanagement of team workload.
Being a project manager, not knowing what your team members are assigned to and how many members are working to their full potential creates a project mess. And, there’s a high chance you’ll fail to leverage the full [benefits of collaboration](<https://fluentboards.com/blog/benefits-of-collaboration/>).
You might assign the same people in multiple tasks, leading to:
  * Stress
  * Anxiety
  * Burnout
  * Team conflicts
  * Project quitting

**Best practices** :
Keep in mind that how strongly you perform as a team would hugely depend on you and your team’s [collaboration skills](<https://fluentboards.com/blog/team-collaboration-skills/>). However, to remove the hassle of work mismanagement, try applying these formulas:
  * Identify your full team potential and assign tasks according to their skillset
  * To balance the workload, review the schedule, sprints, and deadlines
  * Train the employees who are underperforming in a specific skillset
  * Arrange one-on-one or group meetings to know if all employees are on the same page

### Absence of accountability
When every team member takes responsibility for their role and works collaboratively, it increases efficiency and performance. And, your project success won’t be just a gamble – the success percentage will hit the sweet 100%.
If your team is not committed to the project or its goals, in other words, if there’s a lack of accountability – it creates a ripple effect that can drag your project down faster.
**Best practices:**
  * Initially figure out the goals and spread tasks according to your team skillset
  * Incorporate a PM tool to track and monitor assigned project progress
  * Arrange sessions to discuss challenges members are facing while completing the project

### Impractical deadlines
Imagine without proper planning, the project manager agrees to design, develop, manufacture, and launch an entire product within a month. 
Despite all team efforts, there’s a high chance the project will struggle to meet the impractical deadline! And, even if the product is ready, the end product quality might be severely affected.
**Best practices:**
  * Before the project gets up and running plan how much time it’ll need to get the final product
  * Break the task into small chunks and set realistic deadlines that suit the teams
  * Keep checking on the timeline with the use of a PM tool
