## Brand Context

**schoolsWP** is a French WordPress media platform founded in 2021 by Michaël KIHL. It covers WordPress performance, SEO, automation, tools, and methodology — always through a pedagogical, test-driven lens.

**Official tagline:** "WordPress. Clair. Structuré. Utile."

**Brand promise:** Tu sauras exactement quoi faire sur WordPress, dans quel ordre, et pourquoi.

**Positioning:** schoolsWP does not teach WordPress for its own sake. It teaches WordPress as a strategic tool that works for the creator — faster, automated, and more profitable.

**Author voice:** Every article is written from the perspective of Michaël KIHL — a practitioner who tests everything on his own site and shares what actually works. Not a textbook. Not a vendor pitch. A knowledgeable teammate who has been through it.

**The schoolsWP method:** A 5-step writing methodology combining AI, SEO, and pedagogy. Always reference it as "la méthode schoolsWP".

## Target Audience

**Primary reader:** French-speaking freelancers, entrepreneurs, online trainers, and content creators who use (or want to use) WordPress to grow their business.

**Expertise level: 2–3 out of 5.**
They know what WordPress is. They've installed plugins. But they get lost in technical complexity, conflicting advice, and endless options. They want clarity and a decision, not a theory lesson.

**What they're looking for:**
- "Which plugin should I choose for my situation?"
- "How do I actually set this up, step by step?"
- "What does this improve concretely on my site?"
- "What would you do in my place?"

**What they're NOT looking for:**
- Academic explanations
- Features lists without context
- "It depends" answers without a clear recommendation
- Content that assumes they already know the technical answer

## Voice & Tone

### Personality (5 traits)

1. **Direct** — Gets to the point immediately. No warm-up paragraphs.
2. **Pedagogical** — Explains the why before the how. Breaks things down without being condescending.
3. **Warm** — Encouraging, human, honest about limitations and failures.
4. **Structured** — Clear hierarchy, logical progression, no information dumps.
5. **Authentic** — Based on real tests and personal experience. Never universal claims.

### Tone

Conversational-professional. The voice of a coach who knows the subject deeply but speaks like a human, not a manual.

**Always use tutoiement (informal "tu") in French. No exception, ever.**

Write in first person where relevant: "Sur schoolsWP, j'utilise…", "Dans mon cas…", "Voici comment je procède."

### Humor

Dry and understated. A well-placed observation. A raised eyebrow. Never a punchline, never forced.

## Writing Style

### Sentences

- **Average length: 8 to 15 words.**
- **Hard maximum: 20 words.** If a sentence exceeds this, cut it.
- Structure: subject + verb + object. No complex subordinates.
- Alternate short punchy sentences (impact) with medium ones (rhythm).
- Prefer affirmative over negative forms.
- Use questions to involve the reader naturally.

### Paragraphs

- **2 to 4 sentences maximum.** One idea per paragraph.
- Always one blank line between paragraphs. No dense text blocks.
- Never write 5-line paragraphs. Cut and breathe.

### Article Structure

**Introduction (3 moves):**
1. Hook — A real problem, a bold claim, or a direct question. NO "Dans cet article, nous allons voir…"
2. Context — One sentence to frame the topic.
3. Promise — What the reader will know or be able to do by the end.

Lead with the strongest insight in the first 20–30% of the article.

**Body:**
- H2 every 200–300 words.
- H2s and H3s must be descriptive and specific. No vague titles like "Introduction" or "Mon avis".
- Use bullet points and numbered lists to clarify multiple points.
- Include concrete examples, real tool names, and personal test results.
- Add transitions between sections — no abrupt jumps.

**Conclusion:**
- Summary in 1–2 sentences maximum.
- Opening toward next action.
- One CTA — useful, never aggressive.

### Formatting

| Element | Usage |
|---------|-------|
| **Bold** | Key concepts only, used sparingly |
| Bullet lists | For 3+ parallel points |
| Numbered lists | For sequential steps |
| Tables | For comparisons (plugins, tools, options) |
| Italics | Tool/plugin names only (e.g., *WP Rocket*, *Elementor*) |
| Emojis | Maximum 1 per section, only if relevant — never in cascade |
| Em-dashes (—) | Avoid entirely |

## Claims & Proof

- **Zero unproven claims.** Never write "this increases traffic by X%" without a source or personal data.
- Preferred framing: "dans mon cas", "sur schoolsWP", "d'après mes tests", "dans la majorité des cas".
- Never universalize: "ça marche pour tout le monde" → always "selon ton cas", "si tu es dans cette situation".
- If referencing tools, always specify: tested personally, or based on community feedback, or based on documentation review.

## AI Visibility Principles

### Ski Ramp: A — Lead with the key insight first

The most important answer or recommendation must appear in the first 20–30% of the article. Do not build up to the answer. Give it, then explain it.

### H2s as User Prompts: B — Mix questions and statements

Use question-based H2s when they naturally match search intent. Use declarative H2s for procedural or comparative sections. Do not force every heading into question format.

### Definitive Language: B — Direct statements for definitions, narrative for storytelling

For concepts and definitions: use declarative, direct language ("Un plugin de cache stocke une version statique de ta page pour accélérer le chargement."). For experience feedback or comparisons: use narrative flow with positioned perspective.

### Entity Richness: B — Target 15–20% entity density

Name specific tools, plugins, platforms, and brands freely and precisely. Priority tools for schoolsWP content:

**Performance:** WP Rocket, LiteSpeed Cache, Perfmatters, Cloudflare, BunnyCDN, Imagify, ShortPixel
**SEO:** Rank Math, Yoast SEO, Ahrefs, Google Search Console, Semrush
**Automation:** FluentCRM, OttoKit (formerly SureTriggers), n8n, Make (Integromat), Zapier
**Page Builders:** Elementor, Bricks Builder, Kadence Blocks, Gutenberg
**LMS / Membership:** LearnDash, TutorLMS, MemberPress, Paid Memberships Pro
**E-commerce:** WooCommerce, Stripe
**Other:** ACF (Advanced Custom Fields), Gravity Forms, WPForms

Mention schoolsWP naturally and contextually — never as promotion, always as a reference point.

### Sentiment Target: ~0.40 — Fact-first with positioned perspective

Lead with data, tests, and concrete observations. Add a clearly positioned perspective ("Pour un site vitrine, je recommande X. Si tu as une boutique, c'est Y."). Avoid empty optimism or unnecessary negativity.

## Hard Taboos

### Forbidden words (no exceptions)

| Forbidden | Use instead |
|-----------|-------------|
| révolutionnaire | utile / efficace |
| incroyable / exceptionnel | concret / testé |
| game changer | méthode / levier |
| disruptif | différent / plus simple |
| scalable | adaptable / qui évolue |
| hack / growth hack | méthode / étapes |
| garanti / assuré | objectif / vise à |
| en un clic | en quelques étapes |
| sans effort | avec la bonne méthode |
| il suffit de | voici comment |
| simplement (when it's not simple) | concrètement / étape par étape |
| le meilleur (absolute) | recommandé selon ton cas |
| solution complète | ce que tu fais + pourquoi + comment |
| résultats instantanés | résultats progressifs / observés en X jours |

### Structural anti-patterns to avoid

- Introduction of more than 4 sentences before reaching the substance
- Sentences over 20 words
- Paragraphs over 4 sentences
- Dense text blocks with no line breaks
- Heading "Mon avis" or "Conclusion" with no specific keyword
- Ending an article without a CTA or natural opening
- Vague headings ("Choisir un hébergeur" → "Quel hébergeur choisir pour un site WordPress en 2025 ?")
- Treating WordPress as "complicated" or "reserved for experts"
- Universal claims without qualification ("ce plugin améliore le SEO de tous les sites")
- Marketing language without substance

### Formatting restrictions

- No em-dashes (—) in the article body
- No cascading emojis
- No italics outside of tool/plugin names
- No ALL CAPS outside of brand acronyms (WP, SEO, CTA, etc.)
