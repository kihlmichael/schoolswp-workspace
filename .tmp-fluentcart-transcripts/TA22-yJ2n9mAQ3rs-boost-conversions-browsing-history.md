Before we open the dashboard, let's see what's actually happening on your store right now. A customer lands on a store. They browse a few options, jump between pages, and compare things for a while. After some time, they find what they're looking for and place order. From your end, you see a completed sale.

What you don't see is the eight or nine pages they visited before that checkout. You don't know which page built that rust, which page triggered the comparison, or which page finally closed the deal. That missing context is exactly what page browsing history captures, and it's already inside every order waiting for you to look. Setting it up takes about 30 seconds. Upload it to your WordPress ite, activate it, and you're done.

No extra configuration needed. Your store starts tracking customer journeys automatically. Now, let's open an order and see exactly what's captured. On the right side of the order detail page, scroll down to the browsing history panel. The panel header gives you the full summary right away.

Total pages visited, total time the customer spent, the average gap between pages, and their actual active browsing time. Below that is the full journey, every page in sequence with timestamps, dwell time per page, and both the landing page and exit page clearly marked. You can read the entire customer path to purchase at a glance. Simple and useful. Now, the real question is, what do you do with all of this?

Don't open one order and move on. Pull 50 orders and look for patterns. That's where the signal is. Here are the four patterns to look for. Pattern one, the first page.

If one product consistently appears at the start of converting journeys, that's your real seller. That's the page doing the heavy lifting before anything else. Give it more traffic, a stronger call to action, and your best product. Pattern two, the middle pages. Middle behavior tells you everything about comparison and trust.

If customers jump between product pages, they're comparing options. If they visit your shipping or eturns page, that's a trust checkpoint. They needed the reassurance before they could commit. Now you know exactly which objections to address on which page. Pattern three, the penultimate page.

This is the most important pattern in your entire order history, and almost no store owner looks for it. Pull it across your last 100 orders. If one product shows up in that second-to-last position 40 or 50% of your orders, that product is closing your deals. Protect it, strengthen it, drive more traffic to it. That one insight can move your session journey conversion rate more than a month of ad spend.

Pattern four, the timestamps. How long someone spent on a page tells you the quality of their intent. Long dwell time on a product page, deep consideration. They were seriously thinking about it. Four minutes on pricing before checkout, that page needs more clarity, not more content.

Slow means doubt. Fast means confidence. The timestamps show you exactly which is which. You now have something most WordPress store owners don't, a first-party privacy-safe record of exactly how every sale happened. No third-party scripts, no separate dashboards, all built in.
